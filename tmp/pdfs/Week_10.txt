                                  1. Think Aloud
• Users are observed performing specific tasks
• Users are asked to describe what they are doing and why, what they think is
  happening, etc.
• Proceedings are recorded for analysis
• Advantages
   – simplicity - requires little expertise
   – can provide useful insight
   – can show how system is actually use
• Disadvantages
   – subjective
   – selective

2. Audio/Video Narration
• Let the users verbally/visually report their experiences in a free story format.
• Description
   – The users tell a story of their experiences with a prototype; and their story is audio
     recorded. The task assignment can include some topics or questions to support the
     users.
• Strengths
   – The method will give the most important experiences that the users have with the
     product
• Weaknesses
   – Some users may not be comfortable telling about their experiences and the story might
     be very short.
   – The stories need to be transcribed for later analysis

3. Experience Sampling Method (ESM)
• Larson / Csikszentmihalyi [1983]
• ESM is designed to repeatedly request people to
  document and report their thoughts, feelings and
  actions outside the laboratory, within the context
  of their everyday life.
• Process for studying what people
   – Do
   – Think
   – Feel
• Asking individuals to provide systematic self-
  reports
   – Random occasions
   – During specific hours


                                                       12
3. Experience Sampling Method (ESM)
• Interval-contingent sampling
   – Sample on experiences at fixed times
   – Good for time series data
   – Typically, burdensome to subjects
      • They begin to expect prompts
• Event-Contingent sampling
   – Report on experiences based on event of interest
   – Subject must be “cognitively-engaged” into own actions

4. Day Reconstruction Method
Capturing in‐situ data retrospectively
  – Experience Sampling Method
     • High levels of participant burden
     • Not feasible to retrieve rich qualitative accounts
  – How does DRM work?
     • Takes place at the end of the day
        – All experiences are listed in a chronological order
     • Every experience is recalled in relation to preceding ones



                                                                    14
4. Day Reconstruction Method
An Example [Karapanos, 2009]
• 4-week field study on “Adoption of iPhone”
• 6 participants captured their daily experiences at the end of each
  day by
  – Day reconstruction: participants listed all activities of the day that
    somehow related to their iPhone. A brief name and an estimation of time
    spent for each activity.
  – Experience narration: participants were asked to pick the three most
    impactful, either satisfying or dissatisfying, experiences of that day. For
    each of the three experiences, participants were asked to write a story
    that describes in detail the situation, their feelings and their momentary
    perceptions of the product.
