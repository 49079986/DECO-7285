Role-Playing and Bodystorming
• Participants (often design team members) act out specific scenario of
  prototype use; physically act out in a simulated environment
• Users’ needs, goals and problems are identified;
• Better understanding of physical and spatial interactions (Homes,

Role-Playing and Bodystorming
• Let designers step into the shoes of users, simulating emotions,
  behaviours, and decision-making “in situ”
• Help designers understand, emotional and social dynamics
• Playful and engaging, encouraging creativity and group collaboration
• Quick identification of ergonomics issues; embodiment

• Strong commitment to empathy is required
• Role playing can exaggerate scenarios or miss real-world complexity
